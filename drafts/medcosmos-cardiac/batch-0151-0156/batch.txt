explanation:
A physical or electrical stimulus opens the fast sodium channels and then the slow calcium channels of the myocyte: statement A is true.
Rapid repolarization follows the outflow of potassium from the cell: statement C is true.
In the book, extracellular hypokalaemia increases automaticity: statement D is true.
So A, C and D are the answer.
During the effective refractory period the fast sodium channels are inactivated, not only the slow calcium channels, and catecholamines do not act by increasing outward potassium flow.

explanation:
Class 1 antiarrhythmics, the membrane-stabilizing local anaesthetic drugs, act by blocking the fast sodium channels: statement A is true.
Class 3 drugs such as bretylium block the outward potassium current that repolarizes the cell, prolonging the action potential and the refractory period: statement D, as corrected, is true.
Class 4 calcium channel blockers act directly on the SA and AV nodes, slowing the rate and AV conduction: statement E is true.
So A, D and E are the answer.
Class 1 drugs do not shorten the refractory period, and beta-blockers block the effects of circulating catecholamines as well as those of sympathetic nerves.
note: The book's option D reads 'inhibit potassium influx into cells'; class 3 drugs block the outward potassium current (efflux), so the wording is corrected and the key kept.
