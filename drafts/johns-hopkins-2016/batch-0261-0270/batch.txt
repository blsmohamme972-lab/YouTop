explanation:
Intracoronary infusion is the route used most often in clinical trials of cell delivery to the heart: A.
Through a balloon catheter in the infarct artery, the cells are infused while the balloon is briefly inflated a few times, so they can stick to the vessel wall and move into the damaged muscle.
It needs only a standard catheter laboratory, which is why most trials of bone marrow cells after infarction used it.
Injection into the muscle is the other main route: epicardial, through the open chest during surgery, or endocardial, by a catheter needle from inside the ventricle.
Intravenous infusion loses most cells in the lungs, and retrograde infusion through the coronary sinus has rarely been used.
Memory hook: the watering can pours down the crown's slides.
