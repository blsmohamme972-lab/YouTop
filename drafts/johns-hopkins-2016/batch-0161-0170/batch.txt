explanation:
In Loeys-Dietz syndrome, the marionette with twisted strings, aortic aneurysms grow fast and dissect at small sizes.
The drug shown to slow that growth is an angiotensin II receptor blocker such as losartan: B.
Angiotensin II and the TGF-beta signalling it drives, the red darts that puff the bubble up, are blocked right at their receptor, the shield held up at the target.
Beta-blockers, the goalkeeper diving too late, are widely used but are not the book's answer, and doxycycline, diuretics and ACE inhibitors are not either.
Memory hook: the shield at the target stops the darts, and the bubble stops growing.
note: The evidence for angiotensin receptor blockers in Loeys-Dietz syndrome comes mostly from animal studies and small series of patients; the book's answer reflects them.
