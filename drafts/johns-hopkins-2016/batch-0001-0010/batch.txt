explanation:
Functional status helps decide whether a patient can survive a lung resection.
In the shuttle walk test the patient walks back and forth between two cones 10 metres apart, faster at every level.
Fewer than 25 shuttles, that is under 250 metres, is considered a prohibitive operative risk: D is the answer.
A Karnofsky score of 10 percent describes a moribund patient, a very high risk, and a Zubrod score of 0, not 10, is normal.
A drop in oxygen saturation of more than 4 percent during exercise, not 2 percent, is the significant one.

في صباح صاف على ظهر الثاوزند صني، وقف زورو وسيفه مرفوع فوق كيس منتفخ من الدولارات الخضراء.
«أيّها الطبيب الصغير، أأقطع الآن أم أنتظر؟»
فأشار تشوبر إلى عجلة خشبيّة عملاقة، يركض فيها ثور ضخم في قناع شفّاف.
وكان الثور يعدو بكلّ ما فيه من قوّة، والهواء يدخل قناعه ويخرج.

source: توني توني تشوبر
explanation:
Before a lung resection, exercise testing measures the maximal oxygen consumption, VO2max (MVO2).
VO2max is the most specific predictor of pulmonary complications after resection: D is the answer.
A VO2max above 15 to 20 mL/kg/min means a low risk, not a high one.
An FEV1 above 1.5 L for a lobectomy, not 1 L, needs no further testing, and predicted postoperative FEV1 and DLco above 40 percent, not 25 or 20 percent, mark a low risk.

explanation:
Cardiac complications are among the commonest serious complications after thoracic surgery, so cardiac risk is assessed before every resection.
Stair climbing is a simple, useful screen: a patient who cannot climb more than two flights of stairs should have a formal cardiology evaluation: E is the answer.
Diabetes and congestive heart failure are cardiac risk factors that call for further testing.
The first tests are noninvasive; cardiac catheterization is not the initial screen.

quote: «إن لم تخاطر بحياتك، فلن تصنع المستقبل!»
source: مونكي دي لوفي
explanation:
Resuscitative thoracotomy in the emergency department gives its best results after penetrating chest trauma in a patient who loses signs of life in front of the team.
A penetrating anterior chest wound in a patient who becomes tachycardic and then pulseless is that case: D is the answer.
A blunt-trauma patient found pulseless with no electrical activity at the scene, or one with no cardiac activity after a fall, almost never survives it.
A hypotensive patient who still has a pulse belongs in the operating room, not on the emergency department floor.
