explanation:
Any of the four can explain the fall in saturation and blood pressure with a CVP of 22 after an LVAD: all of the above, E.
Right heart dysfunction is the classic cause: the right ventricle cannot deliver blood to the pump, the CVP rises, the LVAD underfills and output falls, and right-to-left flow through a patent foramen ovale lowers saturation.
Tamponade gives the same picture, with high filling pressures and low output.
An obstructed outflow graft or a poorly decompressed left ventricle raises left atrial pressure, causes pulmonary oedema and hypoxia, loads the right ventricle and lowers systemic flow.
Echocardiography sorts them out.

explanation:
A failing Fontan is the condition least likely to be managed successfully with an LVAD: D.
In a Fontan circulation there is no subpulmonary pump: blood flows passively from the venae cavae to the lungs, and failure usually comes from high venous pressure and pulmonary resistance, not only from a weak ventricle.
An LVAD can empty the systemic ventricle but cannot push blood through the lungs, so results are poor.
Dilated cardiomyopathy, postcardiotomy failure, myocarditis with shock and arrest after a Norwood operation are all supported, often successfully, as a bridge to recovery or transplantation.

Leukopenia is not part of the toxicity profile of calcineurin inhibitors: C.
Cyclosporine and tacrolimus block T-cell activation without suppressing the bone marrow, so the white cell count is not lowered; leukopenia belongs to antiproliferative drugs such as azathioprine and mycophenolate.

explanation:
ACE inhibitors are the exception: they are not among the established measures to prevent cardiac allograft vasculopathy, B.
Allograft vasculopathy is a diffuse, concentric thickening of the inner layer of the graft's coronary arteries, driven by immune injury and by risk factors such as lipids.
It is prevented by adequate immunosuppression, by statins, by calcium channel blockers such as diltiazem, and by proliferation signal (mTOR) inhibitors such as everolimus and sirolimus.
