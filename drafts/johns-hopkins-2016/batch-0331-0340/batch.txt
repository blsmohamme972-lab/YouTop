In pulmonary stenosis the pulmonary annulus is either normal or smaller than expected: C.
The typical valve is dome-shaped, its commissures fused, with a small central opening at the top of the dome; a dysplastic valve has thick leaflets and a small annulus.

An open pulmonary valvotomy should extend to the edge of the annulus: C.
Each fused commissure is incised right out to the annulus, so the valve opens fully; stopping short leaves a residual gradient.
Thick, dysplastic leaflets can be partly excised, and a transannular patch is added when the annulus itself is small.
Inotropes are best avoided, since they increase dynamic infundibular obstruction, and systemic pressure is supported rather than allowed to fall.

Broad fistulas between the RV and the coronary arteries can be directly ligated on the surface of the heart: E.
When the coronary circulation does not depend on the RV, a large fistula can be tied off where it runs on the epicardium, removing the steal into the coronary system.
