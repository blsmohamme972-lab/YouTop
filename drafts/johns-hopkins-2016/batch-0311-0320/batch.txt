explanation:
The AV septum refers to the AV component of the fibrous membranous septum: D.
In the normal heart the tricuspid valve is attached a little lower (more apically) than the mitral valve, so part of the septum separates the left ventricle from the right atrium: that part is the atrioventricular septum.
In an AVSD this septum is missing and the junction is common: both valves hang at one level, with a common annulus and a shared valve.
The mitral annulus is not more apical than the tricuspid one; the reverse is normal.

explanation:
The pattern of blood flow between the chambers in AVSD is largely determined by chordal and leaflet anatomy: A.
Where the bridging leaflets are tied down to the crest of the ventricular septum, shunting is only at atrial level (a partial or transitional form); where they float free, shunting is at ventricular level as well.
The ventricular septum is scooped out, more so with a common orifice, and the AV node is displaced posteriorly and inferiorly, toward the coronary sinus.
The left ventricular outflow tract is long and narrow (the "gooseneck"), but obstruction is uncommon.
In the picture, water crosses only above the door where the harp strings tie the leaves down, and below it too where they float free.
Memory hook: the harp strings decide where the water crosses.
