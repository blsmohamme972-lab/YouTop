explanation:
The ostium of a coronary sinus defect is invariably present along the posteroinferior margin of the triangle of Koch: C.
In an unroofed coronary sinus, the wall between the sinus and the left atrium is missing, so the sinus ostium acts as the hole between the atria, at the back-lower corner of Koch's triangle.
A secundum defect comes from excessive resorption of septum primum or a deficient septum secundum, not from too little resorption.
A superior sinus venosus defect lies at the back and top, where the SVC enters, outside the limbus; an inferior one can stream IVC blood into the left atrium and cause cyanosis.

If the ASD is restrictive, the Qp:Qs will rarely exceed 2:1: B.
