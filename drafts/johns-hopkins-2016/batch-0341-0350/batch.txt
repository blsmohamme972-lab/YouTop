explanation:
In the Collett and Edwards system, pulmonary arteries arising from the descending aorta are type IV: D.

Type IV, the "pseudotruncus", is today regarded as pulmonary atresia with VSD and collaterals, not as truncus.

explanation:
An anomalous right subclavian artery arising from the descending thoracic aorta can be ligated and divided in the setting of interrupted aortic arch: B.

The right ventricle is connected to the pulmonary arteries with a valved conduit, usually a homograft; the fundamental goal is to separate the two circulations, and a leaking truncal valve is repaired only when needed.
Mobilizing the descending aorta well, and avoiding tension, protects the left main bronchus from compression.
