An uncomplicated acute type B dissection, with no malperfusion, no rupture, controllable pain and normal pulses, is treated first with intravenous beta-blockade in the ICU, serial blood pressure monitoring and pain control.
Beta-blockers lower the heart rate and the force of ejection, which reduces the stress on the torn wall; they come before vasodilators.
A vasodilator alone causes a reflex tachycardia that raises wall stress, so it is added only after beta-blockade.
Endovascular or open repair is kept for complications: malperfusion, rupture, refractory pain or hypertension, or rapid expansion.

explanation:
In Marfan syndrome, a ratio of maximal aortic root cross-sectional area (cm²) to height (m) above 10 is an indication for prophylactic root replacement; a ratio of 12 means operate.
The ratio corrects for body size and predicts dissection better than the diameter alone in tall Marfan patients.
A root that grows from one yearly scan to the next adds to the case.
Beta-blockers and angiotensin receptor blockers slow growth but do not replace surgery once the threshold is passed.

In this type A dissection the right axillary and the innominate arteries are both dissected, so the best choice is femoral cannulation, deep hypothermic circulatory arrest and retrograde cerebral perfusion.
The usual first choice, right axillary cannulation, is ruled out because the axillary artery is dissected and the innominate arises from the false lumen.
The femoral artery is quick to reach in a patient with tamponade and gives arterial inflow for bypass.
Retrograde cerebral perfusion through the superior vena cava protects the brain during the arrest without cannulating dissected head vessels.

== 25
source: مثل عربيّ
explanation:
A tall young man with an aortic root aneurysm, a bifid uvula, widely spaced eyes, translucent skin, aneurysms of many arteries and generalised arterial tortuosity most likely has Loeys-Dietz syndrome.
Loeys-Dietz comes from mutations in the TGF-beta pathway; its hallmarks are hypertelorism, a bifid uvula or cleft palate, and arterial tortuosity with aneurysms.
Allergic disease such as asthma and eczema is also common in Loeys-Dietz.
Marfan syndrome shares the tall build, long fingers and pectus, but not the bifid uvula, the tortuosity or the aneurysms throughout the body.
The aorta in Loeys-Dietz dissects at smaller diameters, so surgery is offered earlier than in Marfan.

== 26
source: مثل عربيّ
explanation:
A young Marfan patient with an acute type A dissection, a 5.8 cm root, a trileaflet valve and only mild-to-moderate regurgitation is best treated by valve-sparing root replacement by reimplantation (David), with ascending and hemi-arch replacement.
In Marfan syndrome the whole root, sinuses and annulus included, is abnormal and must be replaced; reimplantation also fixes the annulus inside the graft, which prevents later dilation.
The remodelling technique leaves the annulus unsupported, so annular dilation and late regurgitation are more likely in Marfan patients.
A supracoronary graft with valve resuspension leaves the diseased root to dilate again, and a mechanical valve gives up a good native valve for lifelong warfarin.
With a 3.8 cm arch, a hemi-arch replacement is enough.

source: شانكس
explanation:
An acute type B dissection with leg malperfusion, here in a Marfan patient, is complicated and is treated with an endovascular stent graft over the entry tear.
Covering the primary entry tear redirects flow into the collapsed true lumen and restores perfusion of the left leg.
Anti-impulse therapy alone does not reverse established malperfusion.
Open descending replacement in the acute phase carries a high risk of death and paraplegia, and open fenestration does not close the entry tear.
In Marfan syndrome TEVAR is used with caution and is often a bridge, but it is the right first step for a complicated dissection with malperfusion.

explanation:
During CABG in a man with inferior dyskinesis and severe functional mitral regurgitation, the best step after revascularisation is chordal-sparing mitral valve replacement with a bioprosthesis.
Basal inferior dyskinesis tethers the posterior leaflet, and ring repair often fails in this setting; the CTSN trial found more recurrence after repair than after chordal-sparing replacement.
A partial ring does not address the tethering and is a poor choice for functional regurgitation.
At 73, a bioprosthesis avoids lifelong warfarin, and a mechanical valve offers him no advantage.
Repeating the TEE adds nothing: anaesthesia tends to understate regurgitation, so severe regurgitation on TEE is truly severe.

source: مثل عربيّ
explanation:
The main proven advantage of off-pump CABG over on-pump CABG is fewer blood transfusions.
Avoiding the bypass circuit spares the haemodilution and platelet damage that cause bleeding.
Large randomised trials such as ROOBY, CORONARY and GOPCABE found no clear difference in stroke, perioperative infarction or death, and ROOBY found worse one-year graft patency off pump.
Overall hospital costs are similar.
