== 2
source: الدكتورة كوريها
explanation:
After CABG with a preserved ejection fraction, the discharge regimen is aspirin, a beta-blocker, a statin and, for the small pleural effusions, a diuretic.
Aspirin keeps the grafts open, a statin slows disease in grafts and native vessels, and a beta-blocker lowers postoperative atrial fibrillation and protects the heart.
The small bilateral effusions and the fluid gained on bypass call for a short course of diuretic.
An ACE inhibitor is not routine when the ejection fraction is normal and there is no hypertension, diabetes or kidney disease.
Clopidogrel is added after an acute coronary syndrome, not after elective CABG for stable angina.

Memory hook: a good ventricle and two small puddles: asp, keeper, satin, lasso.

== 3
source: فرانكي

note: The book printed option C as "inTA to LAD", a misprint; it is shown here as "in situ left ITA to LAD". The key is unchanged.
explanation:
With an occluded right coronary artery, a 90% LAD and a 60% marginal, the best all-arterial plan is the in situ right ITA to the LAD, the in situ left ITA to the obtuse marginal and a free radial artery to the posterior descending artery.
The radial artery suffers most from competitive flow and spasm, so it goes to the most severely blocked target: the totally occluded right coronary system.
A moderate 60% marginal lesion leaves native flow that competes with a graft, and an in situ ITA tolerates this far better than a radial.
The right ITA reaches the LAD across the front, and the left ITA reaches the marginal on its own pedicle.
Putting the radial on the 60% marginal invites a string sign and graft failure.

Memory hook: the radial goes where nobody fights it.

== 4
source: طرفة بن العبد
explanation:
Basal inferior wall dyskinesis is the strongest preoperative echo predictor of recurrent ischaemic mitral regurgitation after ring annuloplasty.
A dyskinetic basal inferior wall drags the posteromedial papillary muscle outwards and tethers the posterior leaflet, a pull that a ring cannot undo.
Ischaemic regurgitation is Carpentier type IIIb, restricted leaflet motion in systole, so the tethering keeps acting after the annulus is downsized.
Annular diameter and the regurgitant orifice area predict recurrence less well, and the anterolateral wall is not what pulls on the posterior leaflet.
Marked tethering and basal inferior dyskinesis favour chordal-sparing replacement over repair.
In the picture, the silver dancer on the bottom floor steps out, and the harp strings pull the mitre open despite its new collar.
Memory hook: the floor dancer who steps out undoes the ring.

== 5
source: مثل عربيّ
explanation:
After a standard septal myectomy, a provoked residual gradient of 10 mmHg with no mitral regurgitation is an excellent result: give protamine and finish.
Simultaneous needle pressures in the left ventricle and the aorta, with provocation, are the right way to judge the resection.
A provoked gradient this low needs no further resection.
Going back on bypass to resect more risks a ventricular septal defect and heart block for no gain.
With no mitral regurgitation and no systolic anterior motion, there is no reason to touch the mitral valve.
In the picture, the gentle ramp and the shut mitre tell Chopper to call the lion tamer and close the show.
Memory hook: a low gradient and a shut mitre: tame the hare and go home.

== 6
source: الدكتور هيلولوك
explanation:
The first treatment of tricuspid endocarditis in a drug user is intravenous antibiotics.
Most right-sided endocarditis, usually Staphylococcus aureus, is cured by antibiotics alone, and septic pulmonary emboli do not by themselves call for surgery.
Surgery is kept for failure of antibiotics: persistent bacteraemia, right heart failure from severe regurgitation, very large vegetations with recurrent emboli despite treatment, or resistant organisms.
Valvectomy, repair, replacement and suction removal are later choices, not the first therapy.
In an active drug user, any new valve also carries a high risk of reinfection.
In the picture, Chopper's pencil rings the broccoli cards while Zoro's scalpel stays in its sheath.
Memory hook: on the right side, the pencil before the scalpel.

== 7
source: مثل عربيّ
explanation:
After a bioprosthetic tricuspid valve replacement, the preferred antithrombotic plan is a vitamin K antagonist (warfarin) for about three months.
The new sewing ring and leaflets are most thrombogenic until tissue covers them, and valve thrombosis is more common on the slow-flow right side.
After three months, if there is no other reason for anticoagulation, warfarin is stopped.
Antiplatelet therapy alone, single or dual, protects a right-sided bioprosthesis less well in this early period.
Novel oral anticoagulants are not established for the first months after a bioprosthetic replacement.

Memory hook: a new tissue box on the right gets the rat for one season.

A stable, pain-free patient with three-vessel disease after an ST-elevation infarction is best served by CABG more than 72 hours after the onset of pain.

== 9
source: مثل عربيّ
explanation:
A line pressure that stays high and a spreading purple haematoma at the aortic cannula after starting bypass mean an iatrogenic aortic dissection: move arterial inflow to the right axillary artery.
Pumping into the false lumen extends the dissection, so inflow must come from a vessel that feeds the true lumen.
Right axillary cannulation gives antegrade flow into the true lumen and allows brain perfusion if the arch has to be repaired.
Another ascending site, or a larger cannula at the same site, keeps pumping into dissected aorta.
Converting to off-pump surgery does not treat the dissection, which needs repair.
In the picture, Franky pulls the smoking cannon out of the torn climb and pushes a new one in under the right flipper's armpit, where the axe hangs.
Memory hook: when the climb unzips, go in under the arm.

== 10
source: مثل عربيّ
explanation:
New hypokinesis confined to the anterior wall after a LIMA to the LAD means the LIMA graft has failed until proven otherwise: go back on bypass and revise the anastomosis.
The wall moved normally before surgery and only the LAD territory is affected, which points to a technical problem such as a kink, a twist or a poor anastomosis.
Air in the aorta usually enters the right coronary artery and clears with pressure and time; it does not explain an isolated anterior defect with rising vasopressor needs.
A vein graft placed beyond the IMA leaves the faulty anastomosis in place, and a calcium channel blocker treats spasm, which is less likely here.
In the picture, the vine's elbow-like kink at the lad's slide is unpicked and re-sewn while the front dancers sleep.
Memory hook: a sleepy front wall after a LIMA: look at the seam.
