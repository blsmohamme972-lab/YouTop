note: Many centres would take a patient this unstable straight to the operating room and confirm the dissection there with transoesophageal echo; the book's answer assumes a CT can be done at once.
explanation:
When an acute type A dissection is suspected, the book's answer is a stat CT of the chest and abdomen, then operative repair.
CT is fast, widely available and shows the whole aorta: the entry tear, the extent, the branch vessels and any malperfusion, which guides the operation.
Aortography and MR angiography take too long, and MR is unsafe for an unstable, intubated patient.
A transthoracic echo can show aortic regurgitation and an effusion but cannot rule out or map a dissection.
In the picture, the cat slices the orca-shaped loaf at once and Chopper runs straight to the lit operating lamp.
