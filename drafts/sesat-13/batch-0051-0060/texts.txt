An activated clotting time of 300 seconds after a full heparin dose of 450 IU/kg is unexpected, and the first step is to recheck the ACT.
A full dose almost always gives an ACT well above 480 seconds, so a low value is often a sampling or machine error, such as a sample diluted by a flush or a faulty cartridge.
If the repeat ACT is still low, more heparin is given, and if that fails, heparin resistance from antithrombin deficiency is treated with antithrombin or fresh frozen plasma.
Going on to cannulation with an ACT of 300 risks clotting in the circuit.
