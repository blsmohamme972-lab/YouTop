explanation:
In a patient with chronic kidney disease and a potassium of 5.6 having on-pump CABG, the best way to manage the electrolytes during the operation is zero-balanced ultrafiltration.
The ultrafilter on the bypass circuit removes plasma water with its potassium, and an equal volume of potassium-free balanced solution is given back, so the fluid balance stays at zero.
This lowers the potassium and clears other small molecules without drying the patient out.
Continuous haemofiltration is an ICU therapy, HTK cardioplegia adds a large volume without removing the potassium already in the blood, and glucose with insulin shifts potassium only for a short time.
