explanation:
Breathlessness, or only vague retrosternal or epigastric discomfort, in an obese adult is a typical setting, as being overweight is often associated with this hernia.
A mass of fat and bowel lying in front, behind the sternum, is most likely a hernia through the foramen of Morgagni, which is the answer.
The foramen of Morgagni is an anterior, retrosternal defect between the sternum and the costal attachments of the diaphragm; it is usually small, and its sac most often holds omentum and transverse colon.
A Bochdalek hernia is the tempting wrong option, but it is posterolateral and is mainly a defect of newborns, not an anterior mass in an adult.
A pericardial cyst or a large pericardial effusion would appear as smooth fluid, not fat and bowel, and an extralobar sequestration is lung tissue with its own systemic artery.
Because the bowel in its sac can strangulate, a Morgagni hernia should be repaired, through the abdomen or the chest.

quote: «ومع ذلك، ما أبسط الأمر: في يوم واحد، في ساعة واحدة، كان كلّ شيء سيستقيم دفعةً واحدة!»
source: حلم رجل مضحك
explanation:
The oesophagus has three natural constrictions, measured from the upper incisor teeth.
The first, at 15 centimetres, is the cricopharyngeal sphincter: it is the narrowest point, which makes the upper oesophageal sphincter the tempting wrong answer, yet tablets usually pass it.
The second, at 23 centimetres, is where the aortic arch and the left main bronchus cross the oesophagus, and it is the usual site where tablets lodge, which is the answer.
The third, at 40 centimetres, is where the oesophagus pierces the diaphragm, at the lower oesophageal sphincter.
Elderly patients are most at risk, as they take many tablets, often with little water or lying down, and a lodged tablet can ulcerate the wall, which is called pill oesophagitis.

explanation:
The oesophagus lies directly behind the trachea, and lower down the left main bronchus crosses in front of it, so an advanced oesophageal cancer can break through into the airway.
A malignant oesophagorespiratory fistula follows direct tumour invasion, or radiotherapy, laser therapy, chemotherapy or stenting, and it is a devastating complication.
In a large series from Memorial Sloan Kettering, the trachea was involved in 53 per cent of these fistulas, which makes it the commonest site and the answer.
The left main bronchus is the tempting wrong option, but it came a distant second at 22 per cent, and the right bronchus followed at 16 per cent, with lung tissue and multiple sites far less often.
Coughing, especially on swallowing, with repeated chest infections, is the typical sign of the fistula.

source: رسائل من تحت الأرض
explanation:
A paraganglioma is an extra-adrenal phaeochromocytoma, and in the mediastinum it is rare.
Local invasion found at surgical exploration is the parameter that best indicates its malignant potential, which is the answer.
Microscopy does not separate benign from malignant tumours: the mitotic index and staining for chromogranin or leu-enkephalin do not predict how it will behave.
Most mediastinal paragangliomas are non-functional; a functional one causes sustained hypertension, headaches and sweating, and plasma metanephrines detect it.
These tumours do not respond to chemotherapy or radiotherapy, so complete resection, together with any structure they invade, is the only chance of cure.
Lymph node metastases are the tempting option, and current teaching counts them as proof of malignancy, but the book's key is local invasion found at exploration.
note: Current WHO teaching treats every paraganglioma as having metastatic potential and defines malignancy by metastasis, lymph node metastasis included, which makes the option of lymph node metastases arguable; the book's key, local invasion at exploration, follows the surgical series it quotes and is kept.

explanation:

It causes dysphagia, regurgitation and aspiration.
The best operation is diverticulectomy with a long extramucosal oesophagomyotomy, which is the answer.

note: The book itself says the need for a myotomy is still debated, and some surgeons operate only on symptomatic diverticula; most now do diverticulectomy with a long myotomy, often with a partial fundoplication.

فقال سافّا لاهثًا: «معطفك أنيق يا صاحبي، لكنّ أنفاسنا واحدة!»
وعند ناصية الشارع عربة سوداء تنتظرهما كليهما سواءً، يرتعش فانوسها في المطر.
فنظر ياكوف إلى لوحه، ثمّ إلى السيّدين في المطر، وضرب الأرقام بكفّه، وقال:
quote: «وعي الحياة أسمى من الحياة، ومعرفة قوانين السعادة أسمى من السعادة: هذا ما يجب أن نحاربه!»
source: حلم رجل مضحك
explanation:
STICH enrolled patients with ischaemic heart failure, an ejection fraction of 35 per cent or less and dominant anterior dysfunction of the left ventricle.
They were randomised to bypass grafting alone or to bypass grafting with surgical ventricular reconstruction, not to reconstruction alone.
Reconstruction reduced the end-systolic volume index by 19 per cent, against 6 per cent with bypass grafting alone.
Yet symptoms and exercise tolerance improved to a similar degree in both groups, so reconstruction brought no greater improvement in symptoms, which is the answer.
Better exercise tolerance is the tempting wrong option, but it was not found, and the rate of death or hospitalisation for cardiac causes was the same in the two groups.
note: The book calls it STICH II; it is the surgical ventricular reconstruction arm, or hypothesis 2, of the STICH trial, published in 2009. Its patient selection and the size of the volume reduction were criticised, and some surgeons still offer reconstruction to selected patients.
